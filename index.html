BIS  ·  Internal review

Five homepage directions.

Every theme carries identical placeholder content, so what you are comparing is typography, colour, density, and structure — not copy. Three take their visual posture from firms you already know; two are original. Pick one and the rest get deleted.

Not ready to publish. All figures are bracketed tokens such as [±XX]% NAV on purpose, so no plausible-looking number can reach production by accident. There is no performance data, no track record, and no testimonials anywhere in these pages — those are the categories that attract SEC Marketing Rule problems. Everything here needs counsel review before the site goes public.

Meridian

Ref · Citadel

Deep navy, almost no colour, hairline rules instead of cards, and a headline set very large and very tight. The most conservative of the five and the one that reads most like an established multi-strategy firm.

Type
Archivo grotesque + IBM Plex Mono for data
Mood
Restrained, institutional, dark
Risk
Can read as generic if the copy stays thin
Open Meridian

Lattice

Ref · Two Sigma

White, technical, built on a visible grid, with an implied-volatility curve sitting next to the headline. The only theme that puts a chart above the fold — it argues the firm is quantitative before you read a word.

Type
Chivo + JetBrains Mono labels
Mood
Quantitative, bright, engineered
Risk
A chart above the fold invites "where's the data?"
Open Lattice

Sovereign

Ref · Blackstone

Navy and stone with brass hairlines and a transitional serif, centred in the hero the way a bank's annual report is centred. The most traditional direction, and the easiest to make look expensive with photography later.

Type
Newsreader serif + Public Sans
Mood
Classical, establishment, formal
Risk
Reads older; can feel like private banking, not a fund
Open Sovereign

Vantage

Original

Editorial and asymmetric: a 7/5 grid, an optical serif at display size, one vertical rule doing the work six borders would, and a deep teal instead of the usual corporate blue. Closest to a design-led boutique.

Type
Fraunces variable serif + Karla
Mood
Editorial, confident, boutique
Risk
The most opinionated — ages fastest if tastes shift
Open Vantage

Terminal

Original

The trading desk as the brand. Warm black, sodium amber, zero corner radius, monospace headlines, and an exposure board in the hero showing the firm's own Greek limits. The only theme that looks like the thing you actually do all day.

Type
IBM Plex Mono display + IBM Plex Sans
Mood
Operational, dense, unmistakably derivatives
Risk
Allocators may read it as a prop shop, not a fund
Open Terminal
